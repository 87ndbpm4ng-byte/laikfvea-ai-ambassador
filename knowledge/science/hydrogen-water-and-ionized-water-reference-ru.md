## 1. Core definitions

- Molecular hydrogen is `H₂`: a neutral gas molecule.
- `H₂` is not the same as hydrogen ions (`H⁺`), hydroxide ions (`OH⁻`), hydrogen peroxide (`H₂O₂`), atomic hydrogen, or “free electrons.”
- Hydrogen water is water containing physically dissolved molecular hydrogen. It is not a new chemical form of water.
- Ionized water is a broad term. In an electrolysis context, it commonly refers to water streams whose pH and ion balance have changed at different electrodes. It should not be presented as a clinical or therapeutic category without approved evidence.

## 2. Electrolysis and water ionization

In an electrolysis system, an electric current passes through water containing ions that allow conductivity. At the cathode, water reduction may produce molecular hydrogen and hydroxide ions. At the anode, oxidation reactions occur and may form oxygen or other products depending on the water composition and system design.

- Conductivity depends on dissolved ions; very low-mineral water may reduce electrolysis performance.
- Hard water may contribute to scale or deposits. Any source-water requirements, cleaning method, and maintenance interval must come from the applicable approved manual.
- A device using electrolysis does not automatically make all water inputs suitable, safe, or interchangeable.

## 3. What common measurements do and do not show

### pH

- pH describes hydrogen-ion activity in water.
- It does not directly measure dissolved molecular hydrogen.
- It does not by itself demonstrate mineral composition, drinking-water safety, cleaning status, or health effect.

### ORP

- ORP depends on factors including pH, temperature, dissolved gases, ions, electrode condition, measurement method, and elapsed time.
- ORP is not a direct conversion of dissolved-hydrogen concentration.
- A negative ORP result is not, by itself, evidence of a health or therapeutic effect.

### TDS / conductivity

- TDS and conductivity describe aspects of dissolved material or electrical conductance; they do not identify exact composition or safety.
- They do not establish dissolved-hydrogen concentration or prove that water is purified.

### Dissolved hydrogen

- Dissolved molecular hydrogen should be measured with a method intended for that purpose and used according to its protocol.
- pH, ORP, bubbles, taste, and the presence of a device are not substitutes for a direct dissolved-hydrogen measurement.

## 4. Storage and stability boundaries

- Molecular hydrogen can leave water over time.
- Loss can be affected by an open container, headspace, temperature, agitation, repeated opening, and storage time.
- Heating or boiling can accelerate loss of dissolved gas.
- Do not guarantee a concentration after storage unless a product-specific approved protocol and measurement support that statement.

## 5. Water quality and operational boundaries

- An ionizer should begin with water that is suitable for its approved intended use and follows its manual’s requirements.
- Electrolysis or changed pH does not prove removal of microbes, metals, organic contaminants, dissolved salts, or other impurities.
- Do not call a water-ionization device a universal purifier unless that capability is specifically documented in the relevant approved manual.
- Do not infer flow rate, filter life, concentration, cleaning chemistry, temperature limits, or maintenance instructions from this reference; use the product manual.
